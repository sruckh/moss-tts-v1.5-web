01 — Palette

Every color has one job

Nine of the ten swatches carry a role. Pure blue is the exception: it is beautiful and nearly unusable, so it is restricted to decorative gradients where nothing depends on being read. Ratios below are measured against the ink canvas.

Derived values — hovers, tints, borders, muted text — are produced only by color-mix() between two palette colors. That keeps the system honest: paste any computed value into the picker and it lands on the line between two swatches.

02 — Surfaces

Ink lifted toward paper

Depth is a mix ratio, not a shadow. Each step folds more Paper into Ink. Shadows are avoided entirely; on a navy this deep they read as smudges.

03 — Typography

Three faces, three jobs

Bricolage Grotesque sets anything that names something — it has the slightly irregular, cut-metal quality of studio equipment lettering. Instrument Sans carries the reading, including the script itself. IBM Plex Mono handles anything measurable: durations, sample rates, token counts, model IDs.

04 — Space & shape

Four-pixel grid, four radii

Spacing steps: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 72. Corners: 4 for chips and inline marks, 8 for controls, 14 for containers, full round for pills and transport buttons.

05 — Actions

One indigo button per view

Indigo means synthesis — the thing that costs GPU seconds. Everything else recedes. Destructive actions borrow Burnt, outlined rather than filled, because deleting a render is recoverable and shouldn't shout louder than rendering one.

Focus is Olive at 2px with a 2px offset, on every interactive element, in both themes. It is the only place the brightest color in the palette appears on small elements — which is exactly why it works.

06 — Status

Render states and license state

Two independent axes share one badge component. Render state answers "is the audio there yet." License state answers "may this audio ship" — a real constraint when half the model rack is research-only.

08 — Voice library

A voice is a card

Each card carries a name, the model behind it, a fingerprint of its sample, and whether it can be shipped. Selection is a border, not a checkbox — the whole card is the target.

09 — Playback

The spoken line

The signature component. Waveform and script are one instrument: the played portion of the wave turns Teal, the word being spoken right now sits in Olive, and everything already said settles to dim Paper. It is the only place in the product where color moves.

12 — Rules

How to stay inside the palette

Do
  • Keep Olive for live speech and focus. Its brightness is a signal, not a decoration.
  • Fill with Indigo, Purple or Teal only when the text on top is Paper at 15px semibold or larger.
  • Derive every new value with color-mix() between two palette colors.
  • Pair every color-coded state with a word. Color is the second signal, never the only one.
  • Use tabular mono for anything that ticks: duration, characters, GPU seconds.
Don't
  • Set text in pure Blue anywhere. It measures 2.3:1 on Ink and fails every threshold.
  • Put Teal text on Ink below 19px — it needs the large-text allowance or a Paper fill.
  • Introduce a neutral gray. Gray is what happens when you skip the mix step.
  • Animate anything except the playhead, the spectrum, and the render spinner.
  • Use Green for a primary button. Green is permission; Indigo is action.
